By the end of this page you will be signed in to a specific Home — the one you
meant, not whichever one the app happened to be pointing at — from a client or
from a terminal.

> **Availability:** The Home-first Welcome screen and account-based Home discovery are in the current unreleased 0.3 checkout. Stable 0.2 keeps its released Relay-oriented sign-in.

## The one idea behind this screen

Happier signs you in **to a Home**, never just "to Happier". Your Account,
Sessions and settings live on that Home, so the Home has to be settled before the
credential is. Everything the Welcome screen offers is one of two things: a way to
authenticate against the Home that is currently in focus, or a way to change which
Home that is.

A **sign-in service** is an optional extra on top of that. It is an account you
hold with one service — Happier Cloud by default — that remembers which Homes are
yours and can vouch for you when you sign in to them. It is a convenience for
finding your Homes across devices. It never becomes the authority over a Home, and
normal Home traffic never goes through it.

## What you need first

- A client: the desktop app, the web app, or a phone. See [Get the apps](/getting-started/get-the-apps).
- One of: a Home address or pairing link, an account on a sign-in service that already
  knows your Homes, or a computer you are willing to turn into a
  [Personal Home](/self-hosting/self-host-runtime).

## The Welcome screen

The first thing Happier shows is **Welcome.** — and, if this device has never
completed a sign-in, **First time here?** below it. A device that has signed in
before gets a warmer returning greeting instead, and the ordering of the buttons
changes with it.

The Home's own sign-in methods always come first, labelled for what they actually
start:

| You see | It does |
| --- | --- |
| **New here?** | Creates a new Account on the Home in focus. Its subtitle names that Home: *Create a private account on …* |
| **Continue with …** | Signs in through an identity provider this Home enables. |
| **Email and password** | Signs in with the one email and password held by that Home. See [Account security](/accounts/account-security). |
| **Use a key** | Enters an existing recovery key instead of authenticating interactively. |
| **Sign in with certificate** | Client-certificate (mTLS) sign-in, on Homes that require it. |

Underneath those come the sign-in service's own methods, when a service is
offered. They look the same but are subtitled with the service's name, and the
"new here" card among them reads *Create an account with …, then find or add your
Home* — an honest description of what happens next, because creating an account
with a service is not by itself entry to any Home.

Finally, the navigation actions:

- **Scan a QR or paste a Home link** — enter a Home directly from an invite.
- **Use a different Home** — switch the Home in focus before signing in.
- **Choose sign-in service** — only offered when no Home is in focus yet.
- **Create a Personal Home** — subtitled *Create a Personal Home on this computer*.

A returning device reorders this: its direct Home methods come first, **Scan a QR
or paste a Home link** moves up beside them, and account creation drops to the
bottom as **Start fresh — create a new account**. Nothing is hidden; the order
follows what you are most likely to want.

<Callout type="info">
  On a Home that is its own sign-in service, the same provider can legitimately
  appear twice. Happier collapses the duplicate only when the service's identity
  is exactly the Home's, and it always keeps the direct Home row — so a Home
  recovery-key action never disappears behind a service one.
</Callout>

### If the service does not answer

The sign-in service is additive, so a service problem never blocks the Home's own
methods. You will see one of these, each with a **Retry**, and the Home rows stay
usable beneath it:

- **Can't reach your sign-in service** — it did not respond.
- **No sign-in methods are available here** — it answered but offers nothing right now.
- **Sign-in service not supported** — the address is a Home, or it does not offer account sign-in.

## Entering a Home directly

You never have to go through a service. Three direct routes reach a named Home:

<Steps>

### Paste or scan

Choose **Scan a QR or paste a Home link**. A pairing QR shown by another signed-in
device, or its **Show Pairing Link** equivalent, carries the exact Home. Pairing
material is short-lived and single-use — if it expires, generate a new one rather
than reusing it.

In the unreleased 0.3 desktop app, successful pairing also continues through
local machine setup for that Home. Browsers and phones remain client-only.
See [Add another computer with a link](/getting-started/onboarding#add-another-computer-with-a-link)
for service setup, consent and retry.

### Type the address

Choose **Use a different Home**, then enter the **Home address**. This is the
HTTPS route to a Home; the Home's identity is verified separately, so an address
change does not silently become a different Home.

### Use a descriptor

A Home reachable only over Iroh has no public HTTPS address. Its strict connection
descriptor is what reaches it — see the CLI path below.

</Steps>

## Signing in from a terminal

`happier setup` is the headless equivalent of the whole journey. What it asks
depends on what it can already see.

**No usable Home yet** — it offers four choices:

```text
Find my linked Homes (recommended)   Sign in through your sign-in service
Connect to an existing Home
Create a Personal Home on this computer
Exit
```

Choosing **Connect to an existing Home** asks how: **Saved Home**, **HTTPS
address**, or **Home descriptor file** (including Iroh-only Homes).

**A Home already named on the command line** — the menus are skipped entirely:

```bash
happier setup --home <saved-home>
happier setup --home-url https://home.example.test
happier setup --home-descriptor-file ./home.json
```

**A Home already selected but not answering** — setup says so, keeps your stored
sign-in, and offers **Retry**, **Choose another Home**, or **Exit and try later**.
It never substitutes a different Home for the one you chose.

When the Home recommends a sign-in service, setup still asks before contacting it:

```text
How would you like to continue?
  d) Sign in directly to this Home
  s) Use the recommended sign-in service
  x) Cancel
```

Directly is the default. If you pick the service, it lists that service's methods
under **How would you like to find your Homes?**, including **Use an account key**
and **New here? Create an account key**.

To see the steps without running them:

```bash
happier setup plan --home-url https://home.example.test
```

<Callout>
  `--yes` is not unattended sign-in. With an explicitly named Home it writes that
  target, prints the interactive continuation and exits nonzero as incomplete;
  without one it writes nothing. `--non-interactive` and a no-TTY run always write
  nothing.
</Callout>

### Choosing the service the CLI uses

```bash
happier auth service status
happier auth service use <endpoint>
happier auth service logout
```

`status` verifies the selected service's identity and its sign-in role
before naming it. `logout` drops only this CLI's service credential — Homes you
already entered keep their own.

## When it goes wrong

- **The Home is unreachable.** Retry re-checks that same Home. In the app, a Home row
  stays selected while unreachable rather than falling back to another one.
- **The sign-in service says that Home is not linked to your account.** Connect to that
  exact Home directly; linking it is a separate step described in
  [Finding your Homes](/accounts/your-homes).
- **Sign-in succeeded but this computer still needs encryption material.** Setup continues
  into device pairing. See [Device linking and restore](/accounts/device-linking-and-restore).
- **The service answered with a different identity than the one saved here.** Stop and check
  the address before signing in again; Happier will not adopt the new identity silently.
