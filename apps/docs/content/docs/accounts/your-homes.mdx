By the end of this page you will be able to pick up a new device and reach every
Home that is yours without typing a single address — and you will know exactly how
much authority you handed to the service that made that possible.

> **Availability:** Account-based Home discovery is in the current unreleased 0.3 checkout. Stable 0.2 has no Home directory.

## What the directory is, and is not

Your **sign-in service** account — Happier Cloud by default, or whichever service
your Home recommends — can keep a list of the Homes you have linked to it. That
list is the directory. It exists so a second phone or a fresh laptop can find your
Homes instead of making you find them.

Two boundaries are worth stating plainly, because they are what makes this safe to
use:

- **The service is not the authority over a Home.** It can vouch for you when you
  sign in, but each Home decides whether to accept that, issues its own credential,
  and can stop accepting the service at any time.
- **The service is not in the traffic path.** Sessions, transcripts and files go
  between your devices and the Home. Linking a Home publishes enough for discovery
  and delegated sign-in, not its contents.

The directory is also separate from the **saved Homes** on a device. Saved Homes
are local: what this client knows how to connect to. The directory is what your
account knows. Removing a Home from one does not touch the other —
see [Homes](/accounts/multi-server).

## What you need first

- A Home you are already signed in to, on this device.
- An account on a sign-in service. If you have not chosen one, the Welcome screen's
  **Choose sign-in service** action and `happier auth service use <endpoint>` both set it.
  In the app, **Change service** in **Settings → Account** lists Happier Cloud, your
  current service and any Home you use that also offers sign-in, plus **Another
  service…** for an address. **Check** shows what a service offers before anything is
  saved; nothing changes until you choose **Use**. Your sign-in to the previous service
  stays saved on the device, so switching back keeps you signed in.

## Find your Homes

Open **Settings → Account**. The section is named after your sign-in service (for
example **Happier Cloud**). Signed out, it lists what the service does and the ways
it offers to sign in; signed in, it shows who you are signed in as, with **Linked
Homes** below it. If the service cannot find Homes, or cannot be reached, the
section says so and offers **Change service** (and **Retry** when it is
unreachable). The same actions are on the connection controls, so you can reach them
without leaving what you are doing.

The ways to sign in are the ones your service offers: a provider such as GitHub, an
account key, or an email and password. Email and password sign-in opens right in the
section. If the service offers it, **New here? Create an account** asks for your email
first, sends a confirmation link, and you choose your password after opening that link.
**Forgot password?** appears only when the service can email you a reset link.

**Find your Homes** discovers linked Homes and opens the selected or preferred one.
Your existing Homes and current focus stay unchanged while it runs — discovery
adds, it does not replace. Each Home in the list shows its label and address, and
carries the state of this device's relationship with it:

| State | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Connected** | This device holds a working credential for that Home. |
| **Approval needed** | The Home wants an explicit decision from an already-signed-in device before admitting this one. |
| **Connection failed** | The attempt did not complete. **Retry Home connection** tries the same Home again. |

One Home can be marked **Preferred** with **Set as preferred**. A sign-in that does
not name a Home opens the preferred one, or the only one, and otherwise asks.

If your sign-in service is also the Home that holds your Account (as Happier Cloud
can be), signing in to the service can add that Home to your directory
automatically, even if the Account has no Home directory entry yet. The service
adds its own Home on account-service sign-in when the deployment offers `self`
sign-in, can sign directory assertions, and publishes a Home connection descriptor
matching its stable service identity.
With that Home as the only directory entry, **Find your Homes** connects and opens
it without a Home chooser. If this device already has a local Personal Home, that
Home stays saved with its data; signing in does not move or remove it. A different
Home still needs to be linked separately.

If nothing appears, you will see **No linked Homes yet** — link a Home first, then
refresh. If you expected the service's own Home, check that you signed in to the
same service that hosts the Account; the service operator may also need to enable
same-service Home discovery. If your service sign-in has lapsed, the section asks
you to sign in again before it will refresh or connect anything.

## Make a Home available on your other devices

From a device that is signed in to the Home, use **Link** on that Home's row in
**Linked Homes** (*On this device only. Link it so your other devices can find it.*).
Linking lets the service help your other devices find this Home and request access.
Request, not receive: the Home still applies its own admission rules to each new
device.

From the Home's own host, the same link is made with:

```bash
happier home link-account [--home PROFILE]
```

Linking is optional. A Home works perfectly well as a QR code and an address.

If the account or signing key behind the service changes, Happier stops and asks
before replacing the trusted sign-in information saved for that Home. Continue only
if you expected the change.

## Stop delegated sign-in

Linking is reversible, and the reversal is narrower than people expect.

Use **Stop delegated sign-in to this Home** — named for the service when Happier
knows it — or run this on the Home's host:

```bash
happier home unlink-account [--home PROFILE]
```

From then on the Home refuses **new** sign-ins from that service. What it does not
do:

- It does not revoke credentials the service already helped issue. Devices that are
  already signed in keep their access until you sign them out on the Home itself.
- It does not remove the Home from your directory listing. That is the separate
  **Remove from my … Homes** action, which takes the Home out of the list only and
  leaves its local profile, credentials, data and current connection untouched.

So: unlink stops future delegation, remove tidies the list, and signing a device out
on the Home is what actually ends an existing device's access.

## Checking what the service can do

The section's advanced rows report what the selected service actually is, which
matters when you are pointing Happier at one you run yourself:

- **Sign-in service** — the endpoint in use, and the way to change it.
- **Service identity** — its verified stable identity.
- **Home discovery capability** and **Home sign-in capability** — whether this
  endpoint can hold a directory and whether it can vouch for sign-ins at all. An
  address that is a Home, rather than a service, reports no.
